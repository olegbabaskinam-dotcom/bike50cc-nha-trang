Help on the road

Your bike broke down in Vietnam: what a tourist should do

If your bike stalls, the main task isn't to fix it in the middle of traffic but to get to a safe place first. In Vietnamese cities motorbike workshops are everywhere, and most everyday faults are easier to show to a mechanic.

Step 1. Get off the road

Don't stop suddenly. Switch on the indicator or hazard lights, check the traffic behind and move smoothly to the edge. On a fast road, step away from traffic together with your passenger. At night use your phone's torch, but don't stand in front of the bike in the lane.

Step 2. Check the obvious

  • is there fuel;
  • are the ignition and engine kill switch on;
  • is the side stand up, if the model has a cut-off;
  • is the battery flat;
  • is there a fresh puddle of oil or petrol.

Don't take the engine apart and don't touch the hot exhaust. If you smell fuel, see smoke or a noticeable leak, don't try to start the bike again.

How to find a workshop

Search the map for sửa xe máy — motorbike repair — or ask at the nearest café. Show the mechanic a video of the symptom and the bike model. Before the repair starts, ask about the cause and the cost of parts and labour. If an expensive part is replaced, ask to see the old one.

How to get a broken bike there

If the workshop is close and the wheels turn freely, you can carefully push the bike there. For longer distances, ask the workshop to send a mechanic or suitable transport. Don't agree to dangerous towing with a rope behind another bike.

If the bike is rented

First contact the rental shop and send your location, photos and a short video. Don't order major repairs without agreement: the contract may say who chooses the workshop and pays for the work.

If the bike is yours

Keep the seller's or a trusted workshop's contacts, photos of the registration and the plate on your phone. After a repair, write down what was replaced. Regular maintenance is cheaper than emergency repairs — the main intervals are in the article on bike care.

A mini kit for the road

A charged phone, a power bank, some cash, a raincoat and a workshop's contact are more useful than a big toolkit without experience. For a long trip also check the tyres, brakes, lights and fuel level.

In short

Safety first, then a simple check and contact with a mechanic. Don't start the bike if there's a leak or smoke, and don't agree to risky towing.
