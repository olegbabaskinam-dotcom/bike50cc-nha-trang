How to choose a bike helmet in Vietnam

A helmet isn't chosen by colour or by the label on the box. What matters is that it fits, doesn't rotate on your head, fastens properly and shows no signs of an impact. A helmet that's too loose can come off exactly when you need it.

How to find your size

Measure your head circumference with a tape measure about two centimetres above your eyebrows. The manufacturer's chart gives you a starting size, but trying it on is a must: head shapes and inner padding differ.

A one-minute fit check

  1. Put the helmet on and fasten the strap.
  2. Turn your head right and left — the helmet shouldn't lag behind.
  3. Try to push it back and forth with your hands.
  4. Keep it on for 5–10 minutes: even pressure is fine, sharp pain is not.
  5. Check your view, glasses and that you can breathe freely.

Open or closed

An open-face helmet is lighter and cooler in the city, but covers the face and chin less. A full-face or closed model gives more coverage, especially on long roads. A compromise model should keep a good view and a reliable strap.

What to check before paying

  • a smooth shell without cracks or dents;
  • intact inner foam with no crushed spots;
  • a working buckle and strap mount;
  • a clear visor without heavy scratches;
  • the manufacturer's marking and production date.

Don't buy a helmet after a serious fall, even if it looks fine outside. The inner energy-absorbing layer may already have taken the impact.

The passenger needs a helmet too

If you ride two-up, the second helmet must fit the passenger, not be a random spare “two sizes bigger”. About the riding position and the change in braking distance — in riding two-up on a 50cc.

The main thing

The best helmet is the one that fits snugly, covers the right areas and that you actually fasten before every ride. Don't leave the choice until your first ride.
