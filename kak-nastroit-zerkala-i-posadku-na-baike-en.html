How to set up mirrors and riding position on a bike

Badly set mirrors make you turn your head all the time, and a tense position reduces control. Setting up takes a few minutes and matters especially before your first ride on an unfamiliar scooter.

First, sit as you'll ride

Put the bike in a stable position, sit in the centre of the seat and rest your hands on the handlebars without tension. Your back stays natural, elbows slightly bent, shoulders relaxed. Don't lean on the handlebars: the seat carries your weight, and your hands only work the throttle and brakes.

What you should see in the mirrors

The inner edge of each mirror may catch a small part of your shoulder or arm, but most of it should show the next lane and the road behind. The left and right mirrors don't have to show the same picture: together they should widen your view, not show the middle of the road twice.

  1. Adjust the mirrors sitting in your normal position, not standing next to the bike.
  2. Set the horizon roughly in the middle of the mirror.
  3. Move each mirror outwards until your own shoulder is barely visible.
  4. Tighten the mount and check the position again after a short ride.

The blind spot remains anyway

Even wide mirrors don't show all the space beside you. Before changing lanes, check the mirror first, then quickly turn your head towards the manoeuvre. Keep this check short: don't hold your gaze backwards and keep seeing the traffic ahead.

Feet and hands

Your feet rest fully on the floorboard or footrests and don't hang outside. Your knees don't get in the way of turning the handlebars. The wrist on the throttle stays almost straight: a strong bend tires you quickly and can lead to accidentally adding throttle on a bump. Before starting, make sure you can reach both brake levers without changing your grip.

Signs of a bad setup

  • you mostly see your own shoulders in the mirrors;
  • you have to lean your body to see behind;
  • your wrists or shoulders hurt after ten minutes;
  • you can't turn the handlebars fully without hitting your knees;
  • one mirror shakes or slowly drops.

If a mirror won't hold, a lever has play or the position is genuinely cramped, an adjustment alone won't solve it — the mount needs fixing, and it's worth reconsidering the bike's size.

Short conclusion

A good position gives relaxed hands, free steering and confident access to the brakes. Mirrors show the road and the neighbouring lanes, but before a manoeuvre you still need a blind-spot check. Also read how to use a navigator safely and how to brake on a bike.
