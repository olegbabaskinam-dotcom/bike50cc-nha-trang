Long stay and season

A bike for the winter season in Nha Trang: buy, ride, sell

A winter stay in Nha Trang means three, four, six months. That's the period where renting stops being convenient and buying stops being a gamble. Here's a simple calculation in dong and a clear scenario: buy, ride the season, sell before your flight.

Counting the rental

Scooter rental in Nha Trang is priced by the month, and rates depend on the season, the model and how long you take it. Work out your own option with the formula: monthly rate × number of months + deposit. Add two points people usually forget:

  • The deposit — it's frozen for the whole period, and getting it back depends on the bike's condition when you return it.
  • Liability for damage. A scratch on someone else's bike is your money at return, and the owner judges it.

One more catch with rented transport: you don't choose what you ride. You get whatever bike is free — and it may be a different one every month.

Counting the purchase

Our option is your own 50cc bike from stock: running, with a Blue Card (cà vẹt) showing 50cc, an oil change, tyres and brakes checked, handed over in Nha Trang. Prices are in the catalogue.

After that, only running costs: petrol, oil now and then, the occasional small repair. What upkeep costs is covered separately: bike running costs and 50cc fuel consumption.

The third part of the maths people forget

Rental money is gone for good. A bike you bought is sold at the end of the season — and part of the money comes back. That's why an honest comparison looks like this:

Renting: rate × months = spent, zero at the end.
Buying: the bike's price − what you sold it for = the real cost of the season.

No one can name the resale price in advance, and we won't promise one: it depends on the model, condition, mileage and, above all, the papers. But the logic is simple — the longer the season, the more buying wins, because rent grows every month while the bike's value falls slowly.

Separately and honestly: we don't buy bikes back. We don't take the bike back and don't promise a fixed percentage. You sell it yourself — how it's done is in how to sell a 50cc bike in Nha Trang before leaving.

Why a 50cc for the season

In Vietnam a motorcycle licence category is not required for vehicles up to 50 cm³ — that's the main reason for the choice. Without an international licence with the right category, a 110cc scooter becomes a constant risk, while a 50cc doesn't.

The caveat we always mention: ≤50 cm³ in the registration is a necessary condition, but not an exemption from everything else. Traffic rules, helmets, age and the terms of your stay still apply, and current requirements should be checked at the time of purchase. More — licence rules for 50cc.

The whole season scenario

  1. First week. Pick a bike from stock, see it in person (south of Nha Trang, 9:00–21:00), check the Blue Card, pay and ride away on your own bike.
  2. The season. Ride. Change the oil by mileage, watch the tyres and brakes, park with a lock. Useful at the start — the first week with your bike.
  3. 2–3 weeks before your flight. Wash, fresh oil, honest photos, a listing in two or three channels at once.
  4. Handover. Money comes before handing over the original Blue Card, not after.

Who buying won't suit

If you're in Nha Trang for two or three weeks — rent, buying won't pay off in time. If you don't plan to ride yourself at all and use taxis — even more so. Buying makes sense from roughly two to three months, provided you're ready to spend a couple of weeks selling at the end.

In short

For a 3–6 month season, your own 50cc bike with clean papers isn't an expense but a partly refundable purchase: part of the money comes back when you resell, and all that time you ride your own bike without depending on a rental shop.
