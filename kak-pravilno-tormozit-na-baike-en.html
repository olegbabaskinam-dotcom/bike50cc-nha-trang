How to brake properly on a bike

A confident stop doesn't start with squeezing a lever hard, but with the right sequence of actions. On most scooters the right lever works the front brake and the left one the rear, but check this on your specific bike before riding.

Why you need both brakes

When slowing down the weight shifts forward, so the front wheel gets more grip and does a large part of the work. The rear brake alone lengthens the stop and can cause a skid. Grabbing the front brake hard with the handlebars turned is dangerous too. The working habit is to build up pressure on both levers smoothly and together.

Normal braking step by step

  1. Ease off the throttle early and look where you want to stop.
  2. Straighten the bike as much as the situation allows.
  3. Start gently squeezing both levers.
  4. Increase the pressure gradually, don't grab the brake in one jerk.
  5. Put your foot down only when the speed is already minimal.

In an empty dry area it's useful to stop a few times from 20–30 km/h and remember how the levers feel. That shows where real slowing begins and whether a brake sinks.

Bends, sand and wet markings

Do most of your braking before the bend. While leaned over, use only gentle correction: the tyre has less grip in reserve. On sand, metal covers, white road markings and wet tiles every action should be smoother. Increase the distance and keep the bike more upright.

Emergency stop

First quickly close the throttle, straighten the handlebars and firmly build up both brakes. Look not at the obstacle but at the clear path. If a wheel starts to slide, ease off that lever slightly and add pressure smoothly again. A bike with ABS helps avoid locking, but doesn't replace distance and good tyres.

Beginner mistakes

  • riding with fingers constantly dragging the brake;
  • using only the rear brake;
  • grabbing the front lever sharply with the handlebars turned;
  • braking at the last moment before a traffic light;
  • not checking the brakes after rain, washing or a long stop.

Short conclusion

Proper braking means both brakes, an upright bike, a smooth build-up of pressure and a safe distance. After buying, be sure to practise in a safe area. Before riding use our two-minute bike checklist, and for mountain roads read about climbs and descents on a 50cc.
