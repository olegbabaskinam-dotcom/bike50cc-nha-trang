Phone and navigator on a bike in Vietnam

In an unfamiliar city a navigator helps a lot, but the phone mustn't take your eyes off traffic. It's better to prepare the route before starting and make any changes only after stopping completely in a safe place.

Set the route in advance

  • check the destination and the district, not just the place's name;
  • look at the first two or three turns;
  • download an offline map in case of a weak signal;
  • turn off unnecessary notifications;
  • set voice prompts loud enough.

What the holder should be like

The phone should be held at at least four points and not block the dash, the mirrors or the handlebar turn. After fitting, turn the handlebars fully both ways and check the mount doesn't touch anything. A cheap clamp without a safety strap can open on a pothole.

A case or a safety loop is useful, but shouldn't stop the phone from cooling. In direct sun the device overheats quickly, especially while charging.

Rain and charging

Even a water-resistant phone is better put in a sealed case before a heavy downpour. The charging port is especially sensitive to water. Secure the cable so it doesn't catch on the levers or pull tight when you turn the handlebars.

What not to do while riding

Don't type an address, don't reply to messages and don't hold the phone in your hand. If you miss a turn, keep riding calmly and let the navigator re-route. A sudden manoeuvre across traffic is more dangerous than an extra two minutes on the road.

The phone in a car park

Before walking away from the bike, take the phone and the removable part of the mount if it comes off easily. Don't leave the device under the seat in the sun: a closed storage box heats up fast.

A working routine

  1. Built the route and silenced notifications.
  2. Fixed the phone securely.
  3. Turned on voice prompts.
  4. Made a mistake — don't jerk, keep riding.
  5. To change the route, stop off the road.
