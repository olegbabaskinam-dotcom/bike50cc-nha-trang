How to fuel a 50cc bike in Vietnam

Your first fill-up in Vietnam is usually easier than it looks: pull up, say the fuel type and the amount or “full tank”, pay. The main thing is not to guess the fuel by the pump colour and not to pour just anything into an unfamiliar bike.

What A95 and E5 mean

At Vietnamese petrol stations you'll most often see RON 95 petrol (often called A95) and E5 RON 92. They aren't “good” and “bad” petrol, just different fuel types. For a specific bike, the reference is the manufacturer's recommendation or the model's sticker/manual. If you've just bought a used 50cc and aren't sure what was used before, feel free to ask the seller or a mechanic before your first fill-up.

No need to make a religion of it: a normal bike loves timely servicing, clean oil and sensible riding no less than the right pump. But the habit of asking about the fuel type is good protection against an accidental mistake.

What to say to the attendant

At big stations it's usually enough to point at the tank and name the fuel. Useful phrases:

  • Cho em đổ đầy bình RON 95. — “Please fill the tank with RON 95.”
  • Cho em [amount] nghìn RON 95. — “RON 95 for [amount] thousand, please.” For example, năm mươi nghìn — 50 thousand dong.
  • Đầy bình. — “Full tank.”

Fuel prices change, so don't rely on old numbers from articles and chats: look at the display at the actual station and ask for a receipt if you need one.

A short routine for your first fill-up

  1. Stop straight, switch off the engine and open the fuel cap.
  2. Say the fuel type and the amount, or say đầy bình.
  3. Check the nozzle is in the filler neck and not spilling over the plastic or seat.
  4. After filling, close the cap, take your change and calmly ride away from the pump.
  5. If you notice a strong smell of petrol or see a leak, don't ignore it — stop and have a mechanic check the cap/hose.

What not to do

Don't smoke at the pump, don't start the bike while refuelling and don't force it “right up to the neck”. If the bike behaves strangely after buying — starts badly, stalls, leaks or smells of petrol — that's no reason to change the fuel type at random. It needs a diagnosis first.
