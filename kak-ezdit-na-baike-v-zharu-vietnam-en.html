How to ride a bike in the heat in Vietnam

At speed the heat feels weaker, so it's easy to notice dehydration too late. Sun, hot air rising from the road and a snug helmet reduce concentration. A safe ride starts with choosing the time, water and route, not with trying to reach the air conditioning faster.

Choose the time and route

Long rides are easier to plan in the morning or towards evening. In the middle of the day choose a route with clear stops, petrol stations and shops where you can top up water. Don't plan too tightly: heat slows you down and requires extra breaks.

Clothing for the heat

Bare skin burns quickly and offers no protection in a fall. Better are light, pale long-sleeved clothes, closed shoes, gloves and a sound helmet with ventilation. Sunglasses must stay on well and not distort your view, but a tinted visor must not be used after dark.

Water and stops

Drink little and often before you feel strong thirst. On a long ride, water and something to replace electrolytes are useful, but that doesn't replace plain water and rest. Stop in the shade, take off your helmet and let your body cool down. Alcohol before riding is incompatible with safe control and increases dehydration.

Signs you need to stop

  • headache, nausea or sudden weakness;
  • dizziness and slower reactions;
  • cramps, confused thinking or unusual sleepiness;
  • you no longer properly notice mirrors and road signs;
  • you feel you want to “just get there faster” at any cost.

Stop in a safe place, move somewhere cool, drink water in small sips and ask for help. With confusion, fainting or a sudden worsening, you need urgent medical help; don't continue the ride.

What happens to the bike

Before the ride, check the tyre pressure on cold wheels as the manufacturer recommends. Don't let air out of a hot tyre just because the reading went up after the road. Watch for warnings on the dash, a smell of fuel, unusual noises and loss of power. Don't open hot systems and don't touch the exhaust.

Phone and belongings

A smartphone in direct sun can overheat and shut down navigation. Don't cool it suddenly with water or ice: stop in the shade and let it cool naturally. Don't leave aerosols, lighters and sensitive electronics in a closed storage box. Keep documents and water so that the luggage doesn't get in the way of the handlebars.

After parking in the sun

The seat and metal parts get very hot. Before starting, check them carefully by hand without touching the exhaust. Open the storage box carefully and air out the helmet. Ride calmly for the first minutes after the heat: concentration doesn't come back instantly.

Short conclusion

In the heat the winner isn't the fastest, but the one who plans water, shade and stops. Light covering clothing, a sound helmet and a calm route keep you alert. For navigation use the tips from the phone on a bike article, and before starting do the two-minute check.
