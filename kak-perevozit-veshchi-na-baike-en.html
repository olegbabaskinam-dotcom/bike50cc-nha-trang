How to carry things on a bike safely

A couple of bags change the handling less than a suitcase, but anything badly secured can get into the wheel, catch on the handlebars or shift under braking. The rule is simple: the load must be compact, fixed and not interfere with control.

Where to put things

  • Under the seat: the best option for small valuables, if they don't mind heat.
  • On the standard hook: only a light bag that doesn't touch your knees or swing.
  • In a top box: handy for a helmet, raincoat and shopping within the permitted load.
  • On the rear rack: only with reliable straps and no dangling ends.
  • In a backpack: fine for light items; a heavy backpack tires you and changes the balance.

How to distribute the weight

Put heavy items lower and closer to the centre of the bike. Don't hang a lot of weight on one side of the handlebars. A rear load shouldn't stick out far beyond the bike or take weight off the front wheel.

Check the permitted load of your rack and top box. A plastic platform may look strong, but isn't made for a heavy suitcase.

What to secure it with

Use intact ratchet or cam straps or a quality luggage net. Ordinary rope loosens easily, and an elastic hook can snap off. Tuck all loose ends away so they can't get into the wheel, the CVT belt or the chain.

Check before starting

  1. Rock the load by hand: it shouldn't move separately from the bike.
  2. Turn the handlebars both ways.
  3. Check your view in the mirrors.
  4. Make sure the lights work and the plate isn't covered.
  5. Ride the first hundred metres slowly and stop to check again.

How riding changes

With a load, acceleration is weaker, braking distance longer and the bike tips into bends more slowly. Increase your distance and don't try to ride at your usual pace. On a 50cc overloading is especially noticeable.

What's better not to carry yourself

A big suitcase, glass, long objects, heavy appliances and anything that blocks the handlebars or lights are safer sent by taxi or delivery. Saving on one trip isn't worth a fall.

Rain and valuables

A shop bag isn't waterproof packaging. Put documents and electronics in a separate sealed bag. When rain starts, stop off the road to repack.

If you're planning a long trip, first read checking the bike before you start.
