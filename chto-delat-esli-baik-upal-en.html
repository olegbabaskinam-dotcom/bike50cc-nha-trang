After a fall

What to do if your bike falls

Even a fall in a car park can bend a lever, damage a mirror or cause a leak. Don't rush to start the engine. First make sure people are safe, remove the risk of a collision and calmly inspect the bike.

Make the place safe first

Switch off the ignition and step away from moving traffic. If you're injured, dizzy or in strong pain, don't lift the bike yourself — ask for help. On the road it's more important to mark the spot and call for support than to rescue the plastic immediately.

How to lift without a jerk

Make sure the engine is off and the surface isn't slippery. Fold away the stand on the side you'll stand the bike on. Squat, keep your back straighter, grip solid parts of the frame or handlebars and lift smoothly with your legs. Don't pull by the mirror, plastic, rack or hot exhaust. If the bike is heavy, ask a second person.

A quick check

  • no smell of fuel or oil, no fresh fluid under the bike;
  • the wheels turn freely;
  • the handlebars turn without sticking and look straight;
  • the brake levers aren't broken and have their usual travel;
  • the throttle springs back by itself;
  • the mirrors, lights, indicators and brake light work;
  • no plastic, wire or plate touches the wheel.

When you must not ride on

Don't keep riding with a fuel leak, a soft brake, crooked handlebars, a damaged wheel or tyre, a stuck throttle, a crack in a load-bearing part, a burning smell or a strange engine noise. Better to get the bike to a workshop than test the problem at speed.

If the damage looks cosmetic

Start the engine only after the inspection and stand next to the kill switch. Listen to it running on the spot. Then test the brakes at minimal speed in a closed area. Even if the bike rides normally, show it to a mechanic: a hidden shifted mount or a crack shows up later.

Record the situation

Photograph the whole bike, both sides, the place of the fall and the damage. If another vehicle was involved, keep contacts, plates and documents as the situation requires. Don't argue on the road — safety and recording the facts come first.

Short conclusion

After a fall the order is simple: people's safety, ignition off, lifting correctly, checking for leaks and controls. When in doubt, don't ride. If you stopped on the road, use what to do if your bike breaks down, and before your next ride — the two-minute checklist.
