Bike insurance in Vietnam: do you need it for a 50cc

A 50cc bike seems simple: buy it, put on a helmet and go. But on paper it is still a motor vehicle. One of the documents worth keeping in order is third-party liability insurance. It doesn't make riding “risk-free”, but it helps you pass a check properly and not be left alone with the consequences of an accident.

Do you need insurance for a 50cc

In Vietnam, compulsory third-party liability insurance applies to owners of motor vehicles, including motorcycles and xe gắn máy — the category a petrol bike up to 50cc usually belongs to. It's not an “extra option for tourists”, but a separate document for the vehicle.

Rules and requirements can change, so before buying or a long trip check the current terms with an official insurer. The core regulation is Government Decree No. 67/2023/NĐ-CP.

What this insurance covers — and what it doesn't

Compulsory insurance is first of all liability to third parties. It can work when another person, their health or property were harmed because of your bike. It is not comprehensive cover: it doesn't mean the insurer will automatically pay for scratches on your bike, a stolen helmet or any personal repair.

So don't buy a bike and calm yourself with one piece of paper. Real safety is working brakes and lights, a helmet, careful riding, papers and knowing whom to call if something goes wrong.

What to check when buying a used bike

  • whether there's a valid insurance certificate and until what date;
  • whether there's a cà vẹt registration showing the plate number and 49.5/50cc;
  • whether the bike matches what was shown in the papers and the video;
  • how the insurance is transferred or bought anew after the deal.

Don't count on the previous owner's paper automatically solving your issue. If in doubt, it's easier to check with the insurer and get a valid policy on clear terms. It's a small thing compared to the price of the bike and the consequences of an accident.

What to do after an accident

  1. People first. Stop safely and check on everyone involved; if anyone is hurt, call emergency services and the police if the situation is serious or disputed.
  2. Don't leave and don't argue in the heat of the moment. Take photos of the scene, the bikes, the damage, plates and documents. Note the time and location.
  3. Contact the insurer. Use the official number on the insurance certificate and follow their instructions for the claim and inspection.
  4. Keep everything. Messages, photos, receipts, witness contacts and documents may be requested later.

If you're unsure about the language or the circumstances, don't sign unclear papers on the spot. First call someone who can translate and help you figure it out.

What to always carry

For a normal ride keep the bike's registration, a valid insurance certificate and the insurer's contact in an easy-to-find place. Take photos of the documents on your phone, but don't rely on them alone: the originals or a legally accepted format are required by the rules of a given check.
