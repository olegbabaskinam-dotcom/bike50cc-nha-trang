How to ride a bike through narrow streets and markets

In alleys and near markets the road space keeps changing: a pedestrian steps out from behind a stall, a bike pulls away without leaving room, a cart blocks half the lane. What matters here isn't weaving skill but low speed and readiness to stop completely.

Speed must match what you can see

Ride so that you can stop within the clear stretch you can see. If a building corner, a van or a row of parked bikes blocks your view, ease off the throttle in advance. Don't speed up just because an empty gap opened in front of you for a second.

Keep space around the bike

Don't hug parked cars: a door may open and someone may step out between them. Leave room from stalls, animals and children. When passing oncoming traffic, choose a wide spot in advance and stop if needed rather than trying to squeeze through at the same time.

How to use the horn

A short calm beep helps you announce yourself before a blind corner or next to someone who doesn't see the bike. It gives you no priority and doesn't replace braking. A long loud horn often startles a pedestrian and makes their movement less predictable.

Throttle and brakes at low speed

Keep your hands relaxed, look beyond the front wheel and control the bike smoothly. Don't twist the throttle sharply with the handlebars turned. One or two fingers can be ready to brake, but don't keep the lever squeezed. Before a very tight section it's better to stop and judge the width.

Parking at a market

Look for an official car park, an attendant or a row that is clearly supervised. Don't block an entrance, a walkway, a ramp or other bikes' way out. Remember the section or take a photo: staff often rearrange bikes more tightly. Don't leave valuables and documents under the seat.

What is especially dangerous

  • wet tiles, fish, vegetable waste and sand under the wheels;
  • umbrellas, awnings and goods sticking out into the road;
  • bikes reversing out without a full view;
  • children and animals between the rows;
  • turning into a narrow passage without being able to see oncoming traffic.

If it gets too tight

Stop in a safe place, switch off the engine and walk the bike through that section. A navigator sometimes sends you through an alley that formally exists but is awkward to ride. Turning back and taking another route is better than risking damage to the bike or hitting someone.

Short conclusion

A narrow street needs low speed, a wide view and readiness to give way. Don't compete for every gap. Also read about parking in Nha Trang and the guide to setting up your mirrors.
